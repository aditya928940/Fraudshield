What to do if you already sent money or clicked a link

Don't blame yourself. Scammers are very good at tricking people. Act fast and follow these 4 steps in order:

1

Call Your Bank Instantly

Tell them you were scammed. Ask them to block your card and stop any payment that is still pending.

Within the first hour
2

Check Your Credit Report

Look at your CIBIL report for any loan or card you did not apply for. Tell the lender at once.

Free once a year
3

Reset Passwords

Change your email password right away from a safe phone or computer. Log out of all other devices.

Very important
4

Report to Authorities

Call the Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 right away, then report on cybercrime.gov.in.

Creates an official record

What NOT to do after a scam

Panic makes people repeat the mistake. Stay calm and avoid these 6 things:

delete_forever

Don't delete the evidence

Keep the messages, screenshots, call logs and payment receipts. You need them for the bank and the police report.

phone_disabled

Don't call numbers from the message or Google

Scammers also run fake "customer care" numbers. Use only the number on your card, passbook or official app.

money_off

Don't pay a recovery fee

Anyone who promises to get your money back for a fee is another scammer. Real help from your bank and 1930 is free.

screen_share

Don't share OTP, PIN or your screen

Do not install screen-sharing apps like AnyDesk or TeamViewer, and do not read out any new OTP, even to someone who says they are from the bank.

volume_off

Don't stay silent or feel ashamed

Tell your family and your bank. Scammers count on shame. Reporting quickly is what can save your money.

block

Don't reply to or argue with the scammer

Do not threaten, bargain or send "just a little more" money. Save the evidence first, then block them.

Essential digital safety rules for everyone

Scammers want you to panic. These 4 habits help you stay calm and in control.

pause_circle

1. The "Pause Rule"

Real organizations (your bank, the police, Amazon) never demand money right now by threatening arrest or closing your account.

Action: Stop for 10 minutes. Panic is the scammer's biggest weapon.
search_check

2. Check Who Really Sent It

Caller names and sender names can be faked in seconds. Look carefully at the real website name or email address after the @ or in web links.

Action: Never trust the name on the screen. Hang up and call the number printed on your card.
touch_app

3. Don't Tap Links in Messages

Links in unexpected messages often lead to copy-cat websites that steal your passwords and OTPs.

Action: Open your bank's official app, or type the website address yourself.
key

4. Use a Safer Login Code

Codes sent by SMS can be stolen, or tricked out of you on a fake call. For email and banking, use an authenticator app instead.

Action: Use an app like Google Authenticator instead of SMS codes.

Is my personal data or screenshot kept on your servers? expand_more

No. Your image is only used to check the message and is deleted right after you get your result. We don't store your data or sell it to anyone. Tip: it is read by Google's AI, so hide private details first.

What happens to my personal details in the screenshot? expand_more

We do not hide anything for you. Before uploading, please crop or cover card numbers, ID numbers, OTPs and phone numbers.

What should I do if a screenshot is marked as "Caution"? expand_more

"Caution" means we didn't find a known bad link, but the message still looks suspicious (for example, it rushes you or comes from an unknown sender). Don't reply. Contact the company using its official number or app.

Yes! FraudShield is fully responsive. Simply take a screenshot on iOS or Android, tap the upload area above, and select the screenshot directly from your camera roll.
